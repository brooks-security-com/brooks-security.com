I am a security and systems engineer, ten years into a career in IT and six into
security. I have been the engineer writing the security questionnaire and the one filling
it in, the person deciding whether a tool survived contact with our environment and the
person whose deal rode on the result. What has held steady is that I end up owning what I
build: I write the Terraform, I run the log pipeline, and I am in the room when the
auditor asks what happens when it fails.

I started on the operations side. Three years at Schweitzer Engineering Laboratories went
into Linux endpoint security, hardening the fleet to CIS benchmarks with Chef, and
building the standardized Ubuntu image the company ran on. I was the Linux person on a
Windows help desk, which is where I learned that the interesting problems start where the
documentation stops.

Three years at Syxsense went into designing and deploying security automation inside
other people's environments: Terraform and Ansible integrations, vulnerability management
for Linux fleets in regulated on-premises settings, and architecture sessions with
critical infrastructure and government customers. I finished there as Lead Solutions
Architect, having hired and trained the team that carried the work after me. Much of that
job was in front of an audience, from a master class on realtime endpoint management to
talks on the cyber kill chain and zero trust, and a week-long seminar as a visiting
industry partner at VIT-AP University in Vijayawada, India.

Since March 2024 I have been at AvatarFleet as senior security and systems engineer. I
built the security program from nothing, took it through SOC 2 Type 1 and Type 2 with zero
adverse findings, and wrote the Terraform and Ansible that enforce the controls. The
controls are code, not a promise about code. I also built a SOAR on AWS that runs
log-based inference against WAF and ALB logs to identify and shut down adversarial
campaigns, and I own the log estate behind it end to end. The other half of the job is the
commercial gate: the customer security questionnaires and vendor risk reviews that decide
whether an enterprise deal moves.

Alongside that I run Brooks Security, the independent vCISO practice I founded in April
2024. It is the same work for smaller clients: a 90-day SOC 2 program for seed stage
companies, security and operations consulting, and the judgment that comes from having
operated every control I recommend. Policies do not pass audits. People do.

I live in Moscow, Idaho with my wife Saffron and our son Milo. Family comes first, and
that is a constraint rather than a preference. I left a job once rather than do wrong by
the people who worked for me, and there is no job I would turn down if feeding my family
required it.

I travel as much as the calendar allows, and the places I aim for tend to be old. I have
been reading Roman history since I was twelve, so standing inside it is worth the flight.
I train as well, and powerlifting is the habit I have kept longest: the bar does not care
how I feel about it that morning, which is most of the appeal. Long before that I played
hockey goalie for nine years, which is where I learned that holding your ground while
somebody bears down on you is a skill you can practice.

Most of what I know came from having to operate the thing I designed. That is what the
rest of this index is for.
