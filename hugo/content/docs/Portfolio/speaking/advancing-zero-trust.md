# Advancing Zero Trust Priorities

A conversation rather than a demo, recorded with Chris Steffen of Enterprise Management Associates, who co-chairs the Cloud Security Alliance zero trust working group.

This one is deliberately not a product session. There is no console and no walkthrough, because the honest version of the argument is that zero trust is a philosophy and not something you buy. Chris opens by saying he has never encountered an all-in-one zero trust solution, and that once you dig into what the term actually means, you can see why one cannot exist.

## What it covers

Where organisations actually are, which is further along than most think. The compliance projects already on the calendar contain most of the components: identity, device management, data security, logging. The work is joining them up rather than starting over.

Then the distinction that carries the second half, between checkbox compliance and being genuinely audit ready. Those are different standards, and the audit-ready posture is the one that stops every audit being an emergency.

## Worth watching

The passage on passed-down compliance, which explains why a twelve-person company now gets asked for a security plan by a customer that has never asked before. Chris walks through it with a paper clip manufacturer as the example, and the logic lands because it is not about the paper clips. It is about the connection into someone else's network.

Also the exchange on measurement. A dashboard that reports critical findings going up and down during the day is not evidence that the security team is failing. It is evidence that the environment changed. Knowing which of those two you are looking at is most of the job of presenting security to a leadership team.
