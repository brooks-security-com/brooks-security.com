A session for service providers, with Mason Johnson, on turning regulatory pressure into billable work rather than overhead.

Mason opens on the SEC disclosure rule and the pressure it puts on suppliers. The interesting part of that argument is that it does not stop at public companies. A public company has to disclose within four business days, which means everyone it depends on now gets asked to demonstrate their own controls, whether or not they are regulated themselves.

## What it covers

The economics for a managed service provider. Tool sprawl is the cost centre: separate products for patching, scanning and configuration, each with its own agent, its own training, and its own reports that someone has to reconcile by hand. Consolidating those is a margin story before it is a security story.

Then the commercial argument. Proving compliance is the hard part, and prepackaged remediations for CIS benchmarks and Microsoft STIGs turn that difficulty into a service that can be sold and delivered immediately rather than a project that takes a quarter to scope.

## Worth watching

My demo section, which runs against a multi-tenant console rather than a single environment, because that is the shape of the problem for this audience. A SOC 2 audit lands at 93.4% patch efficacy, and the honest framing of a number like that matters more than the number.
