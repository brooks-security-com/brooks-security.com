The monthly Syxsense Master Class, presented with Rob Brown, working through the Lockheed Martin kill chain phase by phase.

The framing I kept coming back to is that the kill chain is not a sequence you either stop or fail. It is a clock. Every control that adds a phase transition is time the response team gets back, and time is the only thing that reliably helps. That reframes the whole exercise from buying products to buying minutes.

## What it covers

The eight phases, from reconnaissance through to actions on intent, and the control that buys back time at each one. Network intrusion detection and prevention on the perimeter, host intrusion detection at delivery, vulnerability management at exploitation, log aggregation and a SOC at installation, segmentation and DNS sinkholes at command and control, and tested offline backups plus cyber insurance at the end.

## Worth watching

The section on counterfeit authentication, which is the difference between a user approving an install they asked for and a user approving something they did not. It is the clearest argument I have seen for limiting local administrator rights, and it does not require the audience to care about security to follow it.

Rob and I also take questions at the end on tracking local administrator account creation and on renaming the built-in Administrator account, which is a small change that removes an obvious first guess for anyone brute forcing a domain.
